Lía Beluma — Book 03 · Intermediate (B1) · Lessons 21–30

Work, money, law, arts, science. You finish this book able to discuss relative clauses,

conditionals, and the passive voice, and to read the Sova-Néla paper on your own.

Lesson 21 · Relative clauses

Objective: describe who and which, without moving anything.

  • Subordinate clauses are plain SVO behind an introducer (canon 05 §1): - ka who/which (subject): Lé misi ka s unréva — the person who is sad. - ta that/whom (object): Lé fura ta mi né féma — the fruit that I ate. - ka-la whose: Lé misi ka-la lavo né rusa — the person whose dog ran off.
  • Reduction: ka s + adjective → adjective before the noun: lé gino ka s béla → lé béla gino.

Drill D3: the girl who is beautiful → lé béla gino · the fruit that I ate → lé fura ta mi né féma.

Lesson 22 · Conditionals

Objective: say if… then… .

  • Conditional: da = if, clause keeps SVO, main clause follows (canon 05 §5, conflict C7 territory).
  • Da lé séra s éla, mi gai po nésa. If the sky is fair, I go home.
  • Counterfactual shading lives in the tense marker of the if-clause: Da mi né kéma … (if I had known…).

Drill D4: If you sing, I am happy → Da tu níva, mi s réva. · If I go, you stay → Da mi gai, tu misa.

Lesson 23 · The passive voice

Objective: say what was done to what.

  • Passive = -ké on the verb; the doer comes in with li (by): Lé mava né féra li siva. The bread was cut by her (canon 04).
  • -ké also marks "done": lé kiva-ké the finished work.

Drill D2: She cut the bread → passive → Lé brena né féra li siva. · I write the contract (active) → Mi vésa lé sova-tésa. → passive: Lé sova-tésa s vésa-ké li mi.

Lesson 24 · Work and money (register: formal)

Objective: talk jobs, salary and banks in the formal register.

  • kivor worker/employee, kéma-kiva study/work of knowing, riva-gava bank, gava money.
  • Formal style (canon 07): keep particles full, no fusion: Mi s kivor. Mi kiva po riva-gava. I work at the bank.

Drill D4: I work at the bank → Mi kiva po riva-gava. · she is a nurse → Siva s éza-kivor.

Lesson 25 · Law and government

Objective: understand the town's public speech.

  • velta city/wall? — confirmed roots: velta-nésa capitol, jura law/right, jura-tésor legislator, sulo-néna order, un-ziva-sula injustice.
  • Jura in compounds: jura-sula rights (great law), misés-jura human rights.

Drill D3: legislator → jura-tésor · capitol → velta-nésa · human rights → misés-jura.

Lesson 26 · The arts

Objective: talk music, song and story.

  • níva sing/song, níva-nélo melody, níva-os choir (song + the -os group suffix), níva-pira musical instrument, sota story, léva-sota love story, kéla key.

Drill D4: melody → níva-nélo · choir → níva-os · musical instrument → níva-pira · the key story → lé kéla sota — gloss: the key/story (tell of the key).

Lesson 27 · Science in the townspeech

Objective: say the words for knowing the world.

  • saro star, saro-kéma astronomy, teleskopa telescope, saro-liso asteroid, planeta planet, koda code, koda-fala bug, néla-gaza steam, luma-saro flashlight.
  • Register: scientific = sova-dika-méro (legal-scientific style, canon 07): keep terms transparent, no slang.

Drill D3: astronomy → saro-kéma · telescope → teleskopa · asteroid → saro-liso · steam → néla-gaza · bug (in code) → koda-fala.

Lesson 28 · Speech reports

Objective: tell what someone said, asked, or believed.

  • téla say/tell, péra ask, kéma-néna believe — all take ta-clauses (canon 05 §2).
  • Siva téla mu ta lé fira wé hura. She told me the bird will fly.
  • Embed one question without kvo: Mi un kéma kvado nivo gai. I don't know when he goes.

Drill D4: He said that you are good → Nivo téla mu ta tu s gavo. · I don't know where the dog is → Mi un kéma kwaras lé lavo s.

Lesson 29 · Discourse particles: humor, surprise, give-and-take

Objective: sound like a native beyond the grammar.

  • hura wow! and fly; koro really? (also bear); kék yes; du no/stop; un not.
  • Idiom bank (canon 07 §3, VTXT 3.5): su lé kúna exactly right, gava-néla worthless, dora kéla breakthrough, vira-soma a busy week, sula soma to be at rest.

Drill D5: write two lines to a friend, each using one idiom. Model: Mi vira-soma sulo-lé. Hura! (my week is busy-fast. Wow!) · Lé dora kéla s bela. (the breakthrough is beautiful.)

Lesson 30 · Reading the paper

Objective: read the Sova-Néla front page (Issue 1) with the glossary.

  • Sections: Lé tava the town, Lé velta the council, Lé sova-dika the court, Lé vira-soma the week, Lé solá weather, Hura Nira small wonders, Méti the sea, Tora-sola opinions.
  • Do the warm-up: find the idiom, the sentence in the passive, and the relative clause in the issue.

Drill D5: summarize Issue 1 in three Beluma sentences. Check your sentences in the translator.
