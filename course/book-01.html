Lesson 1 · The alphabet and the béla accent

Objective: sound out any Beluma word on sight.

  • 23 letters, one sound each: a b d e f g h i j k l m n o p r s t u v w z x (no c/q/y).
  • Vowels: a father, e bless, i see, o for, u food. j = English y, x = ks.
  • The accent (béla) marks the stressed syllable only: néla=water, luma=light.

Drill D1 (read-aloud, then reveal):

  1. réva (happy) · 2. gino (girl) · 3. lavo (dog) · 4. fira (bird) · 5. saro (star). Key: "réva" -> happy, "gino" -> girl, "lavo" -> dog, "fira" -> bird, "saro" -> star.

Lesson 2 · SVO sentence order

Objective: build and read plain sentences.

  • Order is Subject–Verb–Object, like English: Mi léva tu. I love you.
  • Pronouns (canon 02): mi I, tu you, nivo he, siva she, lía we, íos you (pl), lo they.
  • Copula s = am/is/are: Mi s réva. I am happy.

Drill D3 (gap-fill) — key at line end:

  • Tu s ____ . (you are good) → Tu s gavo.
  • Siva s ____ . (she is beautiful) → Siva s bela.

Lesson 3 · Articles

Objective: mark a noun as defined or not.

  • lé = the, o = a/an: lé nésa (the house), o gino (a girl).
  • Ta doubles as "that" and later the complementizer (canon 05).

Drill D2: translate: the dog → lé lavo; a bird → o fira.

Lesson 4 · Tense markers: né (past), wé (future)

Objective: place one marker before the bare verb. No conjugation, ever.

  • Mi gai. I go / I'm going. → Mi né gai. I went. → Mi wé gai. I will go.
  • Three common verbs have everyday irregular pasts: gai→vára (went), s→rá (was), diva→dá (did) — spoken Beluma (canon 07). Formal writing prefers né gai.

Drill D4 (en→be): I ate fruit → Mi né féma fura. · She will sing → Siva wé níva. · I was happy → Mi rá réva.

Lesson 5 · Plural and possession

Objective: mark more than one; mark belonging.

  • Plural = -s: lavos dogs. But o (a/an) never pluralizes — "some houses" = mavé lomas (canon 01 §4).
  • Belonging = -la: mi-la nésa my home · tu-la lavo your dog.
  • Fast speech fuses them: mila my, tila? no — mila = my (also thousand; context picks the sense, canon 01 homophones).

Drill D4: my dogs → mi-la lavos · her bird → siva-la fira.

Lesson 6 · Compounds — vocabulary for free

Objective: coin transparent words from roots; head first.

  • néla water + sula great → néla-sula ocean. féma eat + pira tool → féma-pira spoon.
  • A divider word refines the head; canon 06 governs morphology.

Drill D5 (creative): invent one house-word and one water-word. Read them aloud; check them in the translator (../translator.html).

Lesson 7 · Questions

Objective: ask yes/no and wh-questions.

  • kvo turns a sentence into a question: Kvo tu gai? Do you go?
  • Wh- series start the sentence: kva which/what, kvasi who, kwaras where, kvado when, kvaso why, havi how: Kwaras tu tita? Where do you live?
  • havi tu s? how are you? (how you are).

Drill D3→D4: Are you going home? → Kvo tu gai po nésa? · Where is the dog? → Kwaras lé lavo s?

Lesson 8 · Negation and commands

Objective: say no and give orders.

  • un = not, before the verb: Mi un kéma. I don't know. Mi un wé gai. I won't go.
  • du = negative command: du gai! Don't go! du féma ta! Don't eat that!

Drill D2 (transform to negative): She will go → Siva un wé gai. · Eat that! (IMM) → du féma ta!

Lesson 9 · Numbers

Objective: count, say prices and dates.

  • Classic series (grammar §9): zéra 0, ona 1, tuo 2, tro 3, fóra 4, kvín 5, sixa 6, sita 7, oita 8, noa 9, diza 10, vinta 20, cénta 100, mila 1,000, miliona 1,000,000.
  • Tens/hundreds stack head-first: vinta-kvín 25, cénta-tuo-tro 123. Ordinals: -ji → onaji first.
  • Zero is unmarked plural for count nouns (canon 01): zéra lavo (no dogs).

Drill D3: 27 = vinta-sita; 47 (twenty-twenty-seven) = vinta-vinta-sita; 123 = cénta-tuo-tro; third = troji.

Lesson 10 · Your first conversation, from memory

Objective: hold the canonical exchange without notes.

  • Sala! Havi tu s? — Hello! How are you? · Mi s gavo. Nako. Unas tu? — I'm good. Thanks. And you?
  • Kék yes, ravi goodbye, krosa friend, po nésa home.

Drill D5 (recite): perform both roles aloud. Then write a 4-line variant using kvo tu gai po nésa? and mi wé gai po nésa. Lesson 40 revisits this exchange in the formal register.
