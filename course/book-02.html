Lía Beluma — Book 02 · Elementary (A2) · Lessons 11–20

Market, daily cycle, health, weather, travel. You finish this book able to shop, tell the

time and the weather, describe symptoms, and sit the A2 paper.

Lesson 11 · Aspect: progressive and perfect

Objective: say what is happening and what has happened.

  • Progressive: s + verb--va: Mi s gai-va. I am going (canon 04 §2).
  • Perfect: vóta + verb: Mi vóta gai. I have gone.
  • Past progressive: né s + V--va: Mi né s gai-va. I was going.

Drill D4 (en→be): She is singing → Siva s níva-va. · I have eaten the fruit → Mi vóta féma lé fura.

Lesson 12 · Adjectives and adverbs

Objective: describe and modify.

  • Adjectives sit before nouns: o béla gino a beautiful girl.
  • Any adjective doubles as the noun of that quality: béla = beautiful and beauty.
  • Adverbs: adjective + -lé: vira-lé quickly, réva-lé happily.
  • Comparatives: mesa … ban …: Nivo s mesa sulo ban mi. He is stronger than me (grammar §8).

Drill D2 (make adverbs): gavo → gavo-lé (well) · sulo → sulo-lé (strongly) · réva → réva-lé (happily).

Lesson 13 · The market

Objective: do business politely.

  • níma want, gava-pira buy, gava-géva sell, gava money, nésa-gava shop, riva-gava bank.
  • Count with numbers: tro furas three fruits (plural -s; o never pluralizes).
  • Ask the price: Havi gava? How much? · Téva please, nako thanks.

Drill D1 (role, then reveal):

  • Buyer: Kvo tu gava-géva furas? Do you sell fruit? → yes, and I buy — Kék, mi gava-pira furas.
  • Seller: Mi gava-géva gavo furas. I sell good fruit.
  • Buyer: Mi níma vinta-fóra? no — Mi níma diza furas. I want ten fruits.

Lesson 14 · The daily cycle

Objective: tell time and daily rhythm.

  • vavi morning, vava? no — séra-vavi sunrise, séra-vo sunset, noita night, sumés time/duration, sumés-nira a moment, sova tomorrow.
  • Dates (folk style, as published in the Sova-Néla): sofavi, 12 séli 1247 suso — "Sofavi, day 12 of year 1247".

Drill D3: sunrise → séra-vavi · a moment → sumés-nira · yesterday evening → safa-vo · tomorrow morning → sova-vavi.

Lesson 15 · Weather

Objective: describe the sky.

  • séra sun/daylight/gold, séra-éla fair weather, néva-sula snowstorm, néva-víma sleet, fréta hail, éla clear/fair, vira wind.
  • Sentence pattern: Lé séra s éla. The sky is fair. Néva s. It's snowing.

Drill D4: The sky is clear → Lé séra s éla. · It snows → Néva s. · fair weather → séra-éla.

Lesson 16 · Health

Objective: say what hurts and what helps.

  • éza health/healing, éza-nésa hospital, éza-kivor nurse, fala sickness, fala-séna symptom, fala-kéma diagnosis, finé-fala fever, zanva-meda vaccine.
  • Pattern: Mi s fala. I am sick. Siva né gai po éza-nésa. She went to the hospital.

Drill D3: hospital → éza-nésa · fever → finé-fala · symptom → fala-séna · I am sick → Mi s fala.

Lesson 17 · Travel: going and finding the way

Objective: ask and give directions.

  • gai go, kwaras where, po to/toward, héna see, kéla key/useful.
  • Kwaras lé riva-gava s? Where is the bank? — model answer: Lé riva-gava s na nésa. The bank is at the house.

Drill D4: Where is the bank? → Kwaras lé riva-gava s? · I go to the shop → Mi gai po nésa-gava.

Lesson 18 · Clauses 1 — complements (that)

Objective: say what you know, think, and want.

  • ta = "that": Mi kéma ta nivo s gavo. I know that he is good (canon 05 §2).
  • kéma-néna believe, téla say, péra ask: Nivo téla mu ta tu s béla. He told me that you are beautiful.
  • níma want + bare verb or a ta-clause: Mi níma gai. I want to go. Mi níma ta tu gai. I want you to go.

Drill D4: I believe that the bird will fly → Mi kéma-néna ta lé fira wé hura. · I want you to sing → Mi níma ta tu níva.

Lesson 19 · Clauses 2 — purposes and reasons

Objective: say why and in order to.

  • fao = for / in order to: Mi gai fao soma. I go in order to sleep.
  • kvaso why; answer with a fao-clause or a ta-clause.

Drill D3: I go to the school in order to learn → Mi gai po kéma-nésa fao léro.

Lesson 20 · Modals and polite requests

Objective: express possibility and ask kindly.

  • kima = can / able: Mi kima gai. I can go. Mi un kima gai. I cannot go.
  • Polite requests with téva (please) and kvo … kima …: Kvo tu kima gai vél mi? Can you come with me?

Drill D4: Can you sing? → Kvo tu kima níva? · I cannot go → Mi un kima gai. · please → téva.
