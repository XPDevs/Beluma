0. Why registers exist here

Beluma has no prestige variety imposed from a capital: it has a prestige register.

The same speaker moves through four registers by choice of lexicon, particle density,

and idiom use — never by changing the grammar spine (SVO, no movement, particles).

The four registers are:

RegisterUsesSignature features
Formal (sóva-sula)speeches, letters, diplomacy, teachingV-form tur, full particles, explicit modals, no unmarked idiom
Colloquial (sova-nira)daily talk, family, marketscontractions, dus affirmation, clip-forms, marked slang
Literary (sova-sula-sota)poetry, fiction, songinverted emphasis, metaphor, archaism, onomatopoeia
Legal-scientific (sova-dika-méro)contracts, statutes, science, medicine-ké passives, ta-complements, loanword spacers, citation forms

Nothing in one register is ungrammatical in another — the difference is density and

choice, not rules.

1. Formal register — sóva-sula ("great speech")

Address. V-form tur where colloquial would use tu:

Tur s tóva jura su lé kiva, éza-nésa, vél lé tiva-sula.
You (V) hold the right to work, to health, with the great… → You hold the right to work, to health, and to shelter.

Particles kept full. Negation un, tense né/wé/wés, modals are all stated; no

clipping, no dus fillers. Prepositions sit with their full object.

Idioms. Only idioms marked (formal) in the lexicon. Colloquial metaphor (e.g.

su lé kúna = exactly right) is avoided in favour of the plain phrase.

Hedges use vento (perhaps) + subjunctive da-va, never bare assertion without a

modal when the subject is the hearer's obligation:

Tur gida-nira téla méra-su po lé velta. — → You are requested to inform the state.

2. Colloquial register — sova-nira ("small speech")

Contractions. Fused prep+N like nésana (in the house) and clitic -lé

(quickly) are free; new ones arise in speech before writing.

Affirmation dus. The particle dus ("do/does, indeed") confirms the previous

clause, like Greek δή or English indeed:

Ké, dus — mava lé gava né s un-gava-va-ké. → *Yes, indeed — the money
finances… the store fell into loss.*

Hmm — better example: Ké, dus, mi s gai po lé loma-tava. — *Yeah, right, I'm off

to the park.*

Clip forms. Roots shorten in vocatives and set phrases: fren (friend), brovo →

bro (bro), sala → sa (hi). Clips never change register by themselves; they mark

solidarity.

Slang. Canon 03–06 slang items (vevo, gavi, krosa-sola) appear here almost

exclusively, and are tagged in the lexicon.

3. Literary register — sova-sula-sota ("great-story speech")

Vowel emphasis. Stress + penultimate breaks create a metrical spine: see

literature/poetry/meter.md. Authors may move a stressed word to clause end for the

beat — the canonical "no movement" rule is relaxed only for poetry/song, where the

moved word is the prosodic peak:

Po lé méra gai-a sova-nira? → To the sea goes the little voice — regular form
Sova-nira gai po lé méra.

Metaphor by composition. Compounding is itself the literary engine: an author

coins a transparent compound and the reader hears the image. E.g. saro-liso (star-stone)

= asteroid; a poet writes saro-liso su lé kúna for dazzling thought.

Archaism. misa (stand) for "to remain, endure" and sula in the old sense of

soul give archaic dignity:

Lé sula s niva-va su lé víra. → The soul grows in the green. (old/hymn register)

Onomatopoeia. sivo niva-va s siva-va (the cloud grows while sounding) — sound

symbolism (s-clusters for wind/water) is a literary device, not a dictionary rule.

4. Legal-scientific register — sova-dika-méro ("judge-maker speech")

Voice. Passive -ké + li agent is standard so the legal subject can stay

unstated when the actor is the state:

Lé gava-ziva s róma-ké vél lé sulo-néna nés áda-ké li lé velta. →

Simplify: S dóva-ké li lé velta — It is delivered by the state. Use the pattern

for statutes: un s fáo-ké lé mises-jura — Human rights shall not be (so) violated.

Mises-jura un s vulno-ké li lé velta. → Human rights are not to be destroyed by the state.
Lé konto s dávo-ké li lé banka. → The account is authorized by the bank.

Complement ta carries the content of the clause the law speaks about

(direct statement of obligation):

Lé sulo-néna sa ta: tur gida tóva lé konta ta un s rona-ké.
→ The law says: you must hold the contract so that it is not broken.

Loanword spacers. Where a root is absent, the legal register keeps the

(loanword) term (e.g. prisona, formula) rather than forcing a loose compound —

precision over transparency, canon 06 tier 4.

Citation forms. Quoted verbs use sa + ta + the quoted clause verb-first

(reported speech takes complement, never movement):

Lé dika sa ta: Siva un ziva. → The judge said that Siva did not win.

6. Style in the machine

beluma.js does not switch registers automatically — register is authored. Canon 07

defines how speakers choose, so the analyzer need only: (1) parse multi-word idiom

keys before single roots, and (2) render -ké/ta/li structures faithfully

(Phase 2–3 verified). Authoring guidance lives here; machine behavior in beluma.js.
