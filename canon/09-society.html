Rules of civility (kíra-sova): address someone by role or root-name; use gavo-misi

("good person") when you lack the name; a request is péra-gavo, never a bare command

except among kin; thanks are always answered ("kék, zira." — fine, it's nothing).

3. The town

  • Sova-Nésa is a velta-nira (small state-town). Its folk idiom for its people is kuma-sula — "the great mouth": the crowd that fills the kuma-tava (square). A person of the great mouth is "one of the town-folk."
  • Streets are vima-sula (great paths); alleys are vima-nira; the harbour market at Méra-Tava holds the weekly tava-marketa (market day / fair).
  • Hospitality: a nésa-gai (visit) — the misi-gai (guest) is welcomed by the nésa-hulor (head of the house); neighbours are nésa-krosa.
  • The commons (véta-namo): the river bank, the bridge, the market ground — held by everyone, pledged by the state. A family's kéla-namo (heirloom key) is the door-key; the folk say the Kémenta key is kéla dé véta namo — "a key of every family."

4. The folk calendar

Time is counted, then dressed in weather:

  • Units: diva day · soma week · mava month · suso year · sumés the seasons.
  • Named days: the 7-day loop is named by its greeting — sofavi (morning), safavi (evening), noitara (night) among them. A date is a day-name + the counted step: sofavi, 12 séli 1247 suso ("sofavi, day 12 of year 1247").
  • Seasons by weather (no abstract season words; speakers name the weather-time): the Clear (séra-éla), the Storm (torva), the Snow (néva-sula), the Green (víra-loma).

5. Regional variation

  • sova-nela-nira — a dialect (small speech-stream). Sova-Nésa speech is the standard; the far towns (on the Véné and the coast) keep their own sova-níra (accent) without abandoning the dictionary.
  • nésa-sova — home speech: the in-group idiom families share; coinable by any household, then cleared through the coiner if it spreads.
  • velta-sova — official speech: the state's register, taught in the court and the university (canon 08).
  • The paper is printed po veta-misi — every reader, every register.
