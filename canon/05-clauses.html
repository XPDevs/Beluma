Lía Beluma — Canon 05: Clauses — Relative · Complement · Adverbial · Conditional

Rev 1 (Phase 2). Extends grammar.html §13 into a full clause system. One rule governs

everything: **a subordinate clause is a normal Beluma sentence with an introducer in front

and nothing moved inside.** No wh-fronting, no inversion, no resumptive gymnastics.

1. Relative clauses

Opened right after the noun. The clause keeps plain SVO.

SituationRelativizerExample (with gloss)
subject (who/which)kaLé misi ka s unréva — the person who is sad
object (that/whom)taLé fura ta mi né féma — the fruit that I ate
possessor (whose)ka-laLé misi ka-la lavo né rusa — the person whose dog ran off
place / time (in which)prep + taLé nésa na ta mi tita — the house in which I live
agent/means (by which)prep + taLé pira li ta lé mava né féra — the tool by which the bread was cut

Restrictive vs non-restrictive: same words; writing adds commas, speech a pause.

Siva, ka s gavo, ... — She, who is good, … (parenthetical).

Reduction: when the relative clause is ka s ADJ, drop ka s:

lé gino ka s béla → lé béla gino (the beautiful girl). Already how the NP works.

2. Complement clauses (that-clauses)

The demonstrative ta doubles as the complementizer ("that"), exactly as in English:

Mi kéma ta nivo s gavo. — I know that he is good.
Lé gino kéma-néna ta lé fira wé hura. — The girl believes that the bird will fly.
Nivo téla mu ta tu s béla. — He told me that you are beautiful.

Verbs that license a ta-clause: kéma (know), kéma-néna (believe), téla/sa (say),

péra (ask), sova (speak), héna (see), lóma-pira (find), níma (want — see §4).

3. Embedded questions

Wh-words introduce a subordinate clause without kvo and the clause keeps plain SVO:

Mi un kéma kvado nivo gai. — I don't know when he goes.
Péra siva kwaras lé lavo s. — Ask her where the dog is.
Tu wé héna kvasi né féma. — You will see who ate.

Same wh-series as main questions: kvasi who, kwa what, kva which, kwaras where,

kvado when, kvaso why, havi how.

4. Want / purpose clauses

níma (want) takes a bare verb, or a full clause with ta:

Mi níma gai. — I want to go.
Mi níma ta tu gai. — I want you to go (lit. "I want that you go").
Mi gai fao soma. — I go in order to sleep. (fao = for / in-order-to)
Mi gai fao ta tu kima gai vél mi. — I go so that you can go with me.

5. Adverbial clauses

All are introducer + a full clause. Position: after the main clause (unmarked) or before

it (stylistic). Tense inside the clause is independent.

6. Temporal reference (before / after / since / until)

Built from existing particles and deictics, no new grammatical machinery:

  • after = dé + V--ké: Dé vóta féma, mi né gai. — After eating, I went.
  • before = un-héna + time noun: Un-héna vo, gai nésa. — Before evening, go home. (Clausal "before you go" = da-incipient construction: Un-héna kvado tu gai, ... — Phase 3 will add a dedicated subordinator.)
  • since (time) = dé + time noun: dé safa — since yesterday.
  • until = po + time noun: Mi sora po vo. — I wait until evening.

7. Conditionals — real, hypothetical, counterfactual

Three degrees, one structure: da + clause, main clause. The main clause's

tense + modal carries the degree. tóla (then) may open the main clause.

DegreeProtasis (if-clause)Apodosis (main)Example (gloss)
open / realda + presentpresent or wéDa tu soma, tóla tu wé réva. — If you sleep, you will be happy.
hypotheticalda + presentda-vaDa mi tóva gava, tóla mi da-va gava-pira o nésa. — If I had money, I would buy a house.
counterfactual-presentda + né sda-vaDa mi né s o fira, tóla mi da-va hura. — If I were a bird, I would fly.
counterfactual-pastda + pastné da-vaDa mi né s o fira, tóla mi né da-va hura. — If I had been a bird, I would have flown.

Formula: degrees rise from tense. Present = open/real. né s (was/were) in the

if-clause lifts toward counterfactual. né da-va in the main clause = "would have".

Note: the conditional da is the same token as the subordinating "if"; the

homophonous dá = did (colloquial past of diva). Context disambiguates, as with

koro (bear / really?).

8. Register notes

  • Legal/news register prefers: main clause + kondise/béra subordination, full relative clauses, agentless passives.
  • Colloquial register drops tóla, fronted pragmatics, and contracts the frequent kéma ta (know-that) → proposed fusion kémta (Phase 3 register list).
  • Embedded questions are the litmus test of fluency: formal Beluma keeps the wh-word clause explicit. There is no "do-support" and no inversion — ever.
