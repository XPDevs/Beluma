Lía Beluma — Canon 01: Articles & Definiteness (Rev 1)

lé = the, o = a/an. Both optional (grammar §2). This canon makes their use exact.

1. Optional but meaningful

Articles are pragmatics, not grammar: omitting them never makes a sentence wrong; using

them makes reference clear.

ConstructionMeaningRegister cline
lé + NPdefinite, already-knownwriting, formal
o + NPindefinite, one of manywriting, narrative
no articlegeneric / at large / massspeech, proverbs
Lé nésa s béla. The house is beautiful (that house we know).
Mi wé gai po nésa. I'll go home (generic "home", no article).
Sélo s torva. A/The wolf raids. — without article: wolves, as a kind.

2. Mass & abstract nouns

Mass/abstract nouns take NO article when indefinite:

néla (some) water · gava (some) money · réva joy.

Definite mass keeps lé: lé néla = the water (e.g. the river's water).

3. With possessives & demonstratives

After a possessive or demonstrative the article drops in speech, keeps in formal text:

eka mi-la nésa (this my home) — formal allows lé eka mi-la nésa; colloquial drops lé.

4. Indefinite plural

Plural indefinite = bare plural (no article; -s does the work):

vinos = some/wine-like drinks plural, meaning "wines": Mi né néva féna-sulos.? —

Simplify: o never pluralizes (o lomas is bad). "Some houses" = mavé lomas

("a good few houses"), "a few houses" = mavé-nira lomas (much-little). A dedicated

quantifier for "some" enters with the Phase 3 quantity batch.

5. Generic the / species reading

"Lé" + singular = kind/species in formal writing (like French "le loup" = wolves):

Lé sélo s tora. The wolf attacks (as a species). Optionally véta sélo for "every wolf".
