2. The object/oblique form

  • 1sg object = mu ("me", "to me"). Default post-verb position:
Siva leva mu. — She loves me. (C2 canonical; replaces "leva mi")
Géva mu fura. — Give me fruit.
  • All other persons use the same form as the subject (SVO keeps them apart): nivo, siva, ét, mis, tur, misu, miro, zava.
  • mu also carries the dative ("to me"): Sova mu ta. — Tell me that.
  • Prepositional objects use the plain pronoun: vél mi = with me.

3. Possessives

Determiner (before noun): pronoun + -la.

mi-la my · tu-la your · nivo-la his · siva-la her · ét-la its ·

miro-la their (sg.) · mis-la our (neutral) · mis-vél-tu-la our (incl.) · mis-dé-tu-la our (excl.) ·

tur-la your (pl./formal) · misu-la their (pl.) · zava-la one's.

Independent (stands alone, = mine/yours/…): same word.

Ek tésa-bira s mi-la. — This book is mine.

Rules:

  • Possessor precedes possessed: mi-la nésa = my home.
  • -la is also the general genitive: bino-la fira = the boy's bird.
  • No separate apostrophe-s form; -la covers it.

4. Reflexive, emphatic, reciprocal

  • Reflexive (X acts on X): pronoun + hasa.
Mi héna mi hasa na lé tira-méra. — I see myself in the mirror.
Nivo né féra nivo hasa. — He cut himself.
  • Emphatic (X, in person): mi-la mi, tu-la tu, nivo-la nivo, …
Mi-la mi nés vésa lé nésa. — I myself built the house.
  • Reciprocal: ona-ona = each other / one another.
Mis léva ona-ona. — We love each other.

5. Demonstratives

WordMeaningPosition
ekathis (near speaker)before noun

tathat (remote / in discourse)before noun

Plural of eka/oka by normal rule: eka-s, oka-s.

eka nésa = this house · okas lomas = those trees (by you)

Pronominal use (standing alone): Eka s gavo. = This is good.

ta is also the object-relative "that": le fura ta mi né féma = the fruit that I ate (see Canon 05).

6. Correlative (indefinite) pronoun system

Built transparently from quantifiers + the classifiers -misi (somebody) and -bira (thing):

Quantifierperson (-misi)thing (-bira)
ona (one, some)ona-misi someoneona-bira something
zéra (zero, no)zéra-misi nobodyzéra-bira nothing
ala (any, whichever)ala-misi anyoneala-bira anything
véta (every)véta-misi everyonevéta-bira everything
Véta-misi s gavo na lé nésa. — Everyone is welcome in the home.
Ala-misi kima gai po lé méra. — Anyone can go to the sea.
Zéra-misi un kéma ta. — Nobody knows that.

-la builds the possessive of any correlative: ona-misi-la someone's, véta-misi-la

everyone's, ala-bira-la anything's.

Notes:

  • The "any" quantifier is the dedicated root ala (any / whichever). The old dictionary idiom véta-mída = any stays listed but is not used to build correlatives (because mída primarily = minute, which would mis-analyse mida-misi).
  • Negative contexts double-negate naturally in colloquial Beluma (Zéra-misi un kéma = "nobody knows nothing" = nobody knows anything). Formal writing uses one negative per clause.

8. Politeness & address (register)

  • Familiar: tu (friends, family, children, equals). Default.
  • Formal/polite singular: tur (strangers, elders, customers, officials).
  • Royal/divine (rare, literary): tur-sula (great-you).
  • Verb forms never change for person; politeness lives entirely in the pronoun + register (Full words in formal speech, fusions in colloquial).
