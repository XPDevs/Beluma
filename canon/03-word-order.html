Lía Beluma — Canon 03: Word Order & Sentence Structure

Rev 1 (Phase 1). Formalizes and completes the SVO sketch in grammar.html §2 and §12.

Applies to formal Beluma; colloquial fusions (Canon 09) keep the same order, only

compressed.

1. Core order: SVO

Subject – Verb – Object. Nothing else is required:

Siva leva mu. — She loves me. (S = siva, V = leva, O = mu)
Mi féma fura. — I eat fruit.

Every clause places these three first; everything else attaches around them.

The verb is never inflected for person/number/tense-form — time lives in a pre-verbal

particle, so V is lexically constant.

2. The full particle pipeline

A finite Beluma clause in formal register:


[TOPIC ya]  SUBJECT  (time-word)  (un)  (né | wé)  (MODAL)  VERB  (OBJECT)  (PP house)

Order among pre-verbal particles is fixed:

PositionSlotExamples
0topic yaFura ya, mi né féma. — The fruit, I ate it.
1subjectmi, lé bino, eka misi
2time adverb (optional)safa (yesterday), vo (in the evening)
3negation unmust precede tense marker
4tense né / wéné past · wé future · (none) present
5modalkima can · gida must · sud should · néma may · da-va would
6verbhead-truth of the clause
Mi safa un né kima gai. — Yesterday I could not go.

(S = mi, TIME = safa, NEG = un, TENSE = né, MODAL = kima, V = gai)

Rule: un never sits after the tense marker. wé un is non-canonical (use un wé).

un + né → "didn't"; un + wé → "won't" (this supersedes the old wés = won't,

resolving conflict C1: wés = about to).

3. Noun phrase internal order


(DEMONSTRATIVE) (POSSESSOR) (NUMBER) (ADJECTIVE*) NOUN (RELATIVE CLAUSE)
  • Demonstratives before possessor: eka mi-la nésa = this my home / this home of mine.
  • Possessor before adjective: mi-la béla nésa = my beautiful home.
  • Adjectives stack before the noun, nothing after except the relative clause: ona réva nira lavo = one happy small dog.
  • lé (the) and o (a) are optional determiners that lead the phrase: lé mi-la béla nésa (the my beautiful home) — allowed in formal writing; colloquially speakers drop lé with possessives (see Canon 01).

4. Copular (be) and existence

  • Identity/quality: NP1 s NP2 / ADJ: Mi s kémor. I am a teacher. Lé hula s bélu. The sky is blue.
  • zava impersonality: Zava s gavo ki ... — It is good that…
  • Location uses s + preposition: Lé fira s na lé loma. The bird is in the tree.
  • Existence ("there is") is the verb esto (Phase 2, VTXT 3.3): Esto o loma ra lé nésa. — There is a tree near the house. Un esto o misi na lé tiva. — There is no one in the room. Full treatment in Canon 04 §6 (esera = being/existence stays as the noun).

5. Questions

  • Yes/no: front kvo, keep SVO: Kvo tu gai po nésa? — Do you go home? Kvo siva leva mu? — Does she love me? (auxiliary is not translated; kvo owns the job)
  • Wh-words: front the wh-word, keep SVO after it — no inversion: Kvado tu gai? When do you go? · Kwasi s ta? Who is that? · Kwaras s lé lavo? Where is the dog?
  • Embedded questions (Canon 05 §3): introduced by the same wh-words after kéma/péra/téla; no kvo, no inversion.
  • Tag questions (informal): fallback intonation + koro? = "…, right?/isn't it?": Tu s ésa, koro? You're tired, aren't you?
  • Alternative (or-) questions: front kvo, present options with ora: Kvo tu nima néla ora fura? Do you want water or fruit?

6. Imperatives

  • Basic: verb-initial, subject implicit 2sg: Gai! Go! · Féma! Eat! · Sova po mi! Speak to me!
  • Plural / polite: explicit tur after the verb (or before — both heard; formal puts it first): Gai tur! You all go! / Go, please.
  • Negative: front du, no un needed: Du gai! Don't go! · Du féma ta! Don't eat that!
  • Polite request: verb-initial + téva (please): Dora tura, téva. Open the door, please.
  • Let's: mis + verb-initial sense, or sova ... po mis patterns; formal coinage is V-mis in Phase 2. Interim: Mis gai! We go! (used as "let's go").

7. Adverbs & adjuncts

  • Manner (+ -lé): after the verb, after the object if present: Mi féma gavo-lé. I eat well. Nivo né vésa lé nésa sulo-lé. He built the house strongly.
  • Time words: pre-verbal slot (pipeline slot 2) by default: Mi safa né gai. Yesterday I went. Emphasis → clause-final: Mi né gai safa.
  • Place phrases (PP): after the object: Lé fira hura na lé hula. The bird flies in the sky. Mi vása lé bira su lé tavi. I put the thing on the table.
  • Adverb scope: a clause-initial adverb or PP may precede the whole clause; when it does, no ya needed and SVO follows intact: Safa, mi né gai. Yesterday, I went.

8. Ditransitives ("give")

Two canonical frames, both in use:

  1. Recipient-first: Géva mu lé fura. Give me the fruit. (recipient before theme)
  2. Theme-first: Géva lé fura po mi. Give the fruit to me. (po = to)

Frame 1 is more verbal/daily; frame 2 is narrative/formal. mu fills the recipient slot

in frame 1; other recipients use the full noun/pronoun: Géva lé bino lé fura.

Give the boy the fruit. Nivo né géva lé gino lé firos. He gave the girl the birds.

9. Prepositions

Prepositions precede their noun phrase (head-initial, matching compound order is only in

compounds; phrases are prepositional):

na in/at · po to/toward · dé from · su on/above · un-su under · vél with ·

li by (agent, passive — see Canon 04) · ra near · tera through · trima between ·

fao for · etc. (full registry in h.txt).

10. Comparison

Adjective → comparative mesa … ban, superlative susa…:

Nivo s mesa sulo ban mi. He is stronger than me.
Susa béla gino na lé tiva s siva. The most beautiful girl in the room is her.

(Colloquial alternative: mesa sulo ban → fused mesasulo-ban in fast speech.)

11. Subordinate & relative clauses

  • Relative clauses are postnominal, opened by ka (subject), ta (object), ka-la (possessive); no movement inside the clause (see grammar §13, extended in Canon 05): Lé misi ka s unréva. The person who is sad. · Lé fura ta mi né féma. The fruit that I ate.
  • Adverbial subordinators head their own clause, order free (clause-final is unmarked): kausa because · béza since · da if · béra although · sunés so/therefore · ekse thus · seke then · kondise provided that. Mi un gai, kausa mi s ésa. I'm not going, because I'm tired.
