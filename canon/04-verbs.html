2. Aspect — the efficient system

Beluma reuses two things you already own: the copula s and the suffixes

-va (‑ing) and -ké (done). Aspect is therefore compositional, like English

"be + -ing" and "be + -ed":

AspectConstructionExampleGloss
progressives + V--vaMi s gai-va.I am going
past progressivené s + V--vaMi né s gai-va.I was going
perfect (done going)vóta + VMi vóta gai.I have gone
past perfectné vóta + VMi né vóta gai.I had gone
future perfectwé vóta + VMi wé vóta gai.I will have gone
inchoative (begin to)jála + VMi jála gai.I begin to go
habitual (customarily)véta + VMi véta gai vavi.I (usually) go in the morning
iterative (again)ré- prefixMi ré-gai.I go again

Aspect markers are ordinary verbs taking a bare-verb complement — vóta is literally

"finish", jála "begin", véta "every". This keeps the head-first logic: the aspect

word owns the clause, the main verb is its object.

Mi né s gai-va, kvado tu siva-dora héna mu. — I was going when you telephoned me.
Lé fira né s hura-va na lé hula. — The bird was flying in the sky.
Mi wé vóta kiva fao vo. — I will have finished work by evening.

*(-va and -ké stack with the copula; véta before a verb is an aspect adverb, before a

noun it stays "every". No ambiguity in practice: "vetá misi" = every person, "véta gai"

= usually go.)*

3. The participles (-va, -ké)

FormMeaningUse
V--va-ing, ongoingprogressive (with s); also the verbal noun
V--ké-ed/-en, completed statepassive (with s); also "become ADJ"

-ké on an adjective turns the phrase into has become:

Lé néla s nula-ké. — The water has become cold.
Mi s ésa-ké. — I have become tired.
Lé gino s béla-ké. — The girl has become beautiful.

4. Voice — active / passive

  • Active: normal SVO. Lé bino né féma lé fura. — The boy ate the fruit.
  • Passive: front the object as subject, verb becomes s + V--ké; the doer is introduced with li (by, already in the dictionary):
Lé fura s féma-ké li lé bino. — The fruit is eaten by the boy.
Lé fura né s féma-ké li lé bino. — The fruit was eaten by the boy.
Lé nésa wé vésa-ké li mis. — The house will be built by us.
  • Agentless passive: omit li-phrase. Lé tura s dora-ké. — The door is opened.
  • Perfect passive: vóta s + V--ké: Lé fura vóta s féma-ké. — The fruit has been eaten (is in the eaten state).
  • Register: colloquial Beluma prefers active and the generic zava agent (zava né féma lé fura, "one ate the fruit"). Passive is literary / formal / legal / news-register, exactly like French or German formal text.

Voice mirror: s + V--va = progressive (active), s + V--ké = passive/result.

Two suffix contrasts = two opposed but parallel constructions.

Tense + modal composition (slot order: tense before modal):

Mi né kima gai. — I could go (past ability).
Mi wé gida gai. — I will have to go.
Mi wé kima gai. — I will be able to go.
Mi un wé gida gai. — I won't have to go.
Mi né da-va gai. — I would have gone (counterfactual; see Canon 05).

6. Existential ("there is/are")

New root esto (from esera = being/existence, h.txt):

Esto o loma ra lé nésa. — There is a tree near the house.
Un esto o misi na lé tiva. — There is no one in the room.
Esto-s misu na lé méra. — There are TU them at the sea?? — mark: Esto kima-s … —

plural users: Esto mísé-s na lé tiva. — There are people in the room.

The negation is un esto (there isn't), never zéra esto.

Esto mises na lé tiva. — There are people in the room.

7. Colloquial interplay

The irregular colloquial pasts still apply, prefixed to the aspect stack:

Mi rá s gai-va. — I was going. (rá = was)
Nivo vára vóta féma. — He had finished eating. (vára = went/finished)
Mis dá jála lérn. — We began to learn. (dá = did; see Canon 07)
