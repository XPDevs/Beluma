Lía Beluma — The Dialogue Bank (Phase 5)

Authentic exchanges from the Sova-Nésa world, used by lessons 37, the A1–B1 listening

scripts, and classroom role-plays. Dialect: standard townspeech (colloquial unless marked

form). Each line: Beluma · gloss. Every word is dictionary-locked (VTXT 3.6) — nothing

here invents vocabulary.

1 · At the Méra-Tava market (colloquial)

  • Sala! Havi tu s? — Hello! How are you?
  • Mi s gavo. Nako. Unas tu? — I'm good. Thanks. And you?
  • Mi s réva. Kvo tu gava-géva furas? — I'm happy. Do you sell fruit?
  • Kék, mi gava-géva furas. — Yes, I sell fruit.
  • Mi níma diza furas. — I want ten fruits.
  • Diza furas. Kék nako. — Ten fruits. Yes, thanks.
  • Méra-nako, krosa! — Many thanks, friend!

*Note: kék nako (yes, thanks) is a fixed courteous answer; méra-nako is "sea of

thanks" (VTXT 3.4, warm register).*

2 · Family dinner (colloquial)

  • Kvo tu níma néva? — Do you want a drink?
  • Kék, néla téva. — Yes, water please.
  • Lé fura-sula s gavo. — The feast is good.
  • Kék, mi s réva. — Yes, I am happy.
  • Du vira! Soma. — Don't run! Rest.
  • Nako, krosa. — Thanks, friend.

3 · The phone call (colloquial, clipped)

  • Sala, Rina. Kvasi ta? — Hello, Rina. Who is that?
  • Sala, mi s Míra. Kvo tu s na nésa? — Hello, it's Míra. Are you home?
  • Kék, mi s na nésana. — Yes, I'm at home.
  • Lé kéla s na lé tura. — The key is at the door.
  • Hura! Tu s gavo. — Wow! You're good.

nésana is the spoken fusion of nésa na (at home), lesson 8.

4 · The doctor (formal form)

  • Kvo tu s fala? — Are you sick?
  • Mi s fala. Mi s finé-fala. — I am sick. I have a fever.
  • Du vira. Soma. — Don't hurry. Rest.
  • Mi kéma-néna ta lé éza-kivor s gavo. — I believe the nurse is good.
  • Téva. Gai po éza-nésa na vavi. — Please. Go to the hospital in the morning.

5 · A formal letter to Tora-sola (formal)

  • Sala, lé Sova-Néla. — Greetings, the paper.
  • Mi kéma ta lé Kémenta s kéla. — I know the Kémenta is a key.
  • Lé véné géva lé vavi. — The river gives the morning.
  • Mi níma ta lé tava lóma lé kéla. — I want the town to look at the key.

Model letter (3 sentences to learn by heart, lesson 38):

Sala, lé Sova-Néla. Mi kéma ta lé Kémenta s kéla. Lé véné géva lé vavi.

6 · The child at the counting book (colloquial, teaching)

  • Ona, tuo, tro. — One, two, three.
  • Kvo tu lóma? — What do you look at?
  • Mi lóma o zema. — I look at a goat.
  • Kvo lé zema s béla? — Is the goat beautiful?
  • Kék, lé zema s béla. — Yes, the goat is beautiful.

Using the bank

  • Listening: examiners read dialogue 4 or 5 as the listening script (section 4).
  • Role-play: lessons 13 (market), 16 (doctor), 32 (register), 37 (whole bank).
  • Homework: translate any colloquial dialogue into the formal register (lesson 32 rule: spoken fusions go full — nésana → nésa na).
