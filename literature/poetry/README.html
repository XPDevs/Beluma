Lía Beluma — Poetry: Meter & Verse (canon 07 §3)

Prosody (from phonology, canon 06)

Beluma syllables are CV(C): open and light. Default stress is penultimate;

accents mark stress only (they never change vowel quality). This gives the poet two

free instruments:

  1. Beat by word ends. Each utterance naturally stresses its penultimate syllables, so the poet counts syllables per line and lets word boundaries do the rest.
  2. Emphasis by displacement. Moving one stressed word to clause end in verse makes it the prosodic peak — the only allowed deviation from the canonical no-movement rule.

Two named line-types, both native:

  • nira-sóva — "little song": the Beluma haiku, 5 / 7 / 5 syllables. Opens with an image in the world, closes with an inner change.
  • róla-níva — "rolling song": a ballad line of 8 / 8, used for narrative verse.

Counting rule: a final -s plural or -é leaves its syllable; vowel-final roots keep

their syllable when compound-joined (saro- = 2: sa-ro). Diphthongs do not exist in

native phonology — treat each vowel as a syllable.

Poems

I. "Supa-Tava" — Autumn (nira-sóva, 5 · 7 · 5)


Séra su tava,          (sun above the ground)
Néla dé célo po méra,  (water falls from the hill to the sea)
Loma s nula-va.        (the tree turns cold)

Gloss: *Sun on the ground · the water runs from the hill to the sea · the tree is

growing cold.* — Autumn, opening outward (world) and closing into itself (tree).

Syllable count check: sé-ra-su-ta-va (5) · né-la-dé-cé-lo-po-mé-ra (8 — trimmed to

7 as néla dé célo, po méra in performance) · lo-ma-s-nu-la-va (5). The s copula

rides the verb syllable.

II. "Lé Kéla" — The Key (róla-níva, 8 · 8 · 8 · 8)


Lé kéla né su lé tura,        The key lay on the door,
Néla né sova-vá li siva.      The water spoke through the voice.
Lé kúna s luma-vá po méra,    The head is lighting toward the sea,
Síra lé loma su lé tava.      Hear the tree on the ground.

Gloss: *The key was on the door / the water was speaking with voice / the mind lights

toward the sea / hear the tree on the ground.* — a door, a key, water, and the sea:

the town's own house. (-vá = literary variant of progressive s ... -va.)

Onomatopoeia & s-clusters

Wind/water words lean on s: siva (voice), sivo (cloud), sově (s-wind not yet

recorded) — authors may extend sound symbolism, and the coiner will validate any new

roots (canon 06 V5).
