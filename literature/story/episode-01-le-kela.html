Sova-Nésa Cycle — Episode 1: "Lé Kéla" (The Key)

Register: colloquial narration (canon 07 §2). Past narration uses né + verb

(canon 04); progressive s+V--Va; no movement (canon 03).

Characters: Rina (from rina = red/flower/feel), Míra (from míra = dove), the

town, and the Véné, the river.

Setting: Sova-Nésa, autumn, on the riverbank near the Kémenta bridge.

Lé Kéla


1  Rina né vima ra lé Véné, s fóla-va o kéla.
   Rina PAST-walk near the Current, PROG-forget a key.
   Rina walked the bank of the Véné, half forgetting a key.

2  "Kvasi-la kéla?" sa o misi dé lé kuma-sula.
   "Whose key?" said a person from the crowd.
   "Whose key is that?" piped someone in the market crowd.

3  Un krosa né kéma lé kéla.  Lé kéla s silé-va su lé liso.
   No friend PAST-know the key.  The key PROG-silent on the stone.
   No one knew the key.  It stayed silent on its stone.

4  Rina né lósa lé kéla.  "O kéla héna lé néla," sa siva.  "Dus?"
   Rina PAST-take the key.  "A key sees water," said the voice.  "No?"
   Rina picked it up.  "A key looks at the river," she said.  "Right?"

5  Míra s silé-va.  "Ké, dus.  Kémenta-kéla s dora su lé vavi."
   Míra PROG-silent.  "Yes, indeed.  Bridge-key PROG-open at the morning."
   Míra watched the river.  "Yes — the Kémenta key turns at dawn."

6  Su lé vavi séra, lé kéla s nava-va vél lé Véné.
   On the morning-sun, the key PROG-wash with the Current.
   At sunrise the key bathed with the river.

7  O misi dé lé kuma-sula sa: "Ta-lé kéla róma lé vavi."
   A person from the crowd said: "That key stops the morning."
   Someone in the crowd said: "That key holds the morning back."

8  Míra sa: "Rona.  Lé kéla un róma lé vavi."
   Míra said: "Wrong.  The key NEG-stop the morning."
   "Wrong," said Míra.  "The key does not stop the morning."

9  "Su lé vavi séra, lé Véné s luma-va, lé kéla s silé-va."
   "On the morning-sun, the Current PROG-light, the key PROG-silent."
   "At sunrise the river lights up, and the key stays silent."

10 "Lé Véné géva lé vavi," sa Míra.  "Lé kéla s tóva-va lé tura."
   "The Current gives the morning," said Míra.  "The key holds the door."
   "The river gives the morning," said Míra.  "The key holds a door."

Folio note. The cycle recycles core roots on purpose (vima, héna, dora,

luma, silé, róma, géva, tóva) so later episodes build familiarity. The

morning-is-given-by-the-river motif is now canon for Sova-Nésa: the Kémenta key is an

ordinary door-key, and the town's myth is a human story, not a mechanism. kuma-sula

("the great mouth") is the folk idiom for the town's people / the crowd — a person

dé lé kuma-sula is "of the town-folk" (dictionary-locked in VTXT 3.8, canon 09 §3).
