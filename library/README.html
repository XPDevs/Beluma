Lía Beluma — Literature & Media Project (Canon 07 suite)

Phase 4 builds the corpus that makes Beluma a lived language: a shared town, a paper,

poems, stories, and books — all originally composed in Beluma, glossed for learners.

The town: Sova-Nésa ("Speech-Home")

Sova-Nésa is a river town on the Véné (the Current). Its name is transparent to

every speaker: house of speech. The town is the one place in Beluma fiction where all

four registers are heard daily — the paper, the courts, the market, and the bedtime

stories. Characters recur across genres the way people do in a town.

Settled names (proper nouns; see library/encyclopedia/sova-nesa.md):

  • Véné — the river that runs through town (from véné = river/current/flow).
  • Sova-Néla — the weekly paper (from sova speech + néla the stream; cf. the word for language, sova-nela). "The Stream."
  • Méra-Tava — the harbor market (sea-ground), on the river mouth.
  • Kémenta — the oldest stone bridge (knowledge-ston… kéma+múna), the meeting place.

Style rules applied here (canon 07)

  • Beluma-first: each work is composed in Beluma; English glosses are interlinear teaching aids, never the source text.
  • Register-aware: dialogue is colloquial; the paper's editorial news prose is formal; statutes quoted in the paper are legal-scientific.
  • Multi-word idiom keys (e.g. su lé kúna) come from the h.txt idiom batch — if the analyzer lacks one, the work still reads; it just glosses long-form.

Counting for the model

The corpus rule of thumb: **every sentence in every dossier file counts toward the word

budget**. Phase 5 (education) will drill from these texts, so consistent glosses here

are a curriculum signal.
