§04 Pronouns — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: Normative. Beluma pronouns do not mark case by inflection — three of

them have suppletive subject/object forms; the rest are invariable, and possession

is always the suffix -la.

Rules:

  1. The 1 sg object form is mu (suppletive): Mi léva mu. — I love me. Everywhere else subject and object forms are identical.
  2. Possession is always pronoun + -la, written solid: mi-la nésa my house. The possessive pronoun still precedes its noun (§03.2); it never stands alone except predicatively: La nésa s mi-la. — The house is mine.
  3. The reflexive/emphatic is the possessive + the bare pronoun (mi-la mi). It is used for -self meanings and for emphasis; Beluma has no separate reflexive root.
  4. mis-vel-tu = we, including you (inclusive we); mis-de-tu = we, not you (exclusive we). These are lexicalised compounds used where English is ambiguous; ordinary mis is unmarked.
  5. zava = one / people / generic person — the indefinite pronoun: Zava né gai. — Someone went. / Zava un néma. — One may not. After véta, zava gives everyone: Véta zava s gavo. — Everyone is good.

4.2 Address

  • tu = one person, familiar or equal; tur = plural you, and polite singular (the 2 pl form doubles as honorific, as in French vous).
  • Formal writing addresses a named individual as tur + surname or title: Tur Kema, …
  • Vocatives take no article: Siva, gai! — Siva, go!
  • There are no gendered or honorific third-person forms: nivo, siva, misu are neutral descriptions; use tur when politeness about a person requires distance.

4.3 Demonstratives

FormMeaningFunction
ekathis (near speaker)determiner + pronoun
okathat (near listener)determiner + pronoun
tathat (far)determiner + pronoun + relative "that" (§03.5)
  • As determiners they sit first in the noun phrase (§03.2): eka bóla loma *this beautiful tree*.
  • As pronouns they stand alone: Eka s mi-la. — This is mine.
  • ta as a determiner and ta as a relative word never collide: a relative ta always follows its noun (loma ta …), a demonstrative ta always precedes its noun (ta loma).

4.4 Correlatives and indefinites

  • mis-vel-tu, mis-de-tu — inclusive/exclusive we (§4.1.4).
  • véta every/each + noun, zava someone/one (§4.1.5), véta-mída any.
  • Question pronouns (kvasi who, kwa what …) belong to §07; relative pronouns (ka, ta, ka-la) belong to §03.5. Neither is listed here — Beluma keeps the three series (interrogative / relative / demonstrative) formally separate, unlike English who/that/this.

4.5 Pro-drop

Dropping a subject pronoun is informal and requires an unambiguous verb

context — in practice only when the antecedent is the current topic:

  • Standard/Formal: Mi gai po nésa. (full sentence)
  • Informal: Gai po nésa. — (I) go home. (only in reply)

Formal texts MUST keep the subject of every clause; imperative clauses are of

course subjectless by nature (§06.7). Object pronouns are never dropped.
