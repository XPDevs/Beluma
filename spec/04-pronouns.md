# §04 Pronouns — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: **Normative.** Beluma pronouns do not mark case by inflection — three of
them have suppletive subject/object forms; the rest are invariable, and possession
is always the suffix `-la`.

## 4.1 Personal pronouns

| Person | Subject | Object | Possessive (`-la`) | Reflexive / emphatic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 sg | `mi` I | `mu` me | `mi-la` my | `mi-la mi` myself |
| 2 sg | `tu` you | `tu` you | `tu-la` your | `tu-la tu` yourself |
| 3 m sg | `nivo` he | `nivo` him | `nivo-la` his | `nivo-la nivo` himself |
| 3 f sg | `siva` she | `siva` her | `siva-la` her | `siva-la siva` herself |
| 3 n sg | `ét` it | `ét` it | `ét-la` its | `ét-la ét` itself |
| 1 pl | `mis` we | `mis` us | `mis-la` our | `mis-la mis` ourselves |
| 2 pl | `tur` you | `tur` you | `tur-la` your | `tur-la tur` yourselves |
| 3 pl | `misu` they | `misu` them | `misu-la` their | `misu-la misu` themselves |

Rules:

1. The 1 sg object form is **`mu`** (suppletive): `Mi léva mu.` — *I love me.*
   Everywhere else subject and object forms are identical.
2. Possession is always pronoun + `-la`, written solid: `mi-la nésa` *my house*.
   The possessive pronoun still precedes its noun (§03.2); it never stands alone
   except predicatively: `La nésa s mi-la.` — *The house is mine.*
3. The reflexive/emphatic is the possessive + the bare pronoun (`mi-la mi`). It is
   used for *-self* meanings and for emphasis; Beluma has no separate reflexive root.
4. `mis-vel-tu` = *we, including you* (inclusive *we*); `mis-de-tu` = *we, not you*
   (exclusive *we*). These are lexicalised compounds used where English is
   ambiguous; ordinary `mis` is unmarked.
5. `zava` = *one / people / generic person* — the indefinite pronoun:
   `Zava né gai.` — *Someone went.* / `Zava un néma.` — *One may not.*
   After `véta`, `zava` gives *everyone*: `Véta zava s gavo.` — *Everyone is good.*

## 4.2 Address

- `tu` = one person, familiar or equal; `tur` = plural *you*, **and** polite
  singular (the 2 pl form doubles as honorific, as in French *vous*).
- Formal writing addresses a named individual as `tur` + surname or title:
  `Tur Kema, …`
- Vocatives take no article: `Siva, gai!` — *Siva, go!*
- There are no gendered or honorific third-person forms: `nivo`, `siva`, `misu`
  are neutral descriptions; use `tur` when politeness about a person requires distance.

## 4.3 Demonstratives

| Form | Meaning | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `eka` | this (near speaker) | determiner + pronoun |
| `oka` | that (near listener) | determiner + pronoun |
| `ta` | that (far) | determiner + pronoun + relative "that" (§03.5) |

- As determiners they sit first in the noun phrase (§03.2): `eka bóla loma` *this
  beautiful tree*.
- As pronouns they stand alone: `Eka s mi-la.` — *This is mine.*
- `ta` as a determiner and `ta` as a relative word never collide: a relative `ta`
  always follows its noun (`loma ta …`), a demonstrative `ta` always precedes its
  noun (`ta loma`).

## 4.4 Correlatives and indefinites

- `mis-vel-tu`, `mis-de-tu` — inclusive/exclusive *we* (§4.1.4).
- `véta` *every/each* + noun, `zava` *someone/one* (§4.1.5), `véta-mída` *any*.
- Question pronouns (`kvasi` who, `kwa` what …) belong to §07; relative pronouns
  (`ka`, `ta`, `ka-la`) belong to §03.5. Neither is listed here — Beluma keeps the
  three series (interrogative / relative / demonstrative) formally separate, unlike
  English *who/that/this*.

## 4.5 Pro-drop

Dropping a subject pronoun is **informal** and requires an unambiguous verb
context — in practice only when the antecedent is the current topic:

- Standard/Formal: `Mi gai po nésa.` (full sentence)
- Informal: `Gai po nésa.` — *(I) go home.* (only in reply)

Formal texts MUST keep the subject of every clause; imperative clauses are of
course subjectless by nature (§06.7). Object pronouns are never dropped.
