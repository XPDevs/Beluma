# §19 Education, Culture, and Everyday Life — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: **Normative** for patterns; the word list is Informative.

## 19.1 Education

| Concept | Beluma |
| --- | --- |
| education | `eduko` / `kéma-kiva` |
| school | `lernejo` / `kéma-nása` |
| university | `universitato` |
| student | `studento` / `lernanto` |
| teacher | `instruisto` / `kémor` |
| lesson | `lesiono` / `livo` |
| course | `kurso` |
| exam | `ekzameno` / `sak-sula` |
| grade | `noto` / `míra-kéma` |
| diploma | `diplomo` / `kéma-tésa` |
| library | `biblioteko` |

A subject is named `X-kéma` (*knowledge of X*): `téra-kéma` *geography*,
`saro-kéma` *astronomy*, `matematika-kéma` *mathematics*. A school level is
`X-nása`: `baza-nása` *primary school*, `sula-nása` *university-level*.

## 19.2 Curriculum ladder (informative)

| Level | Beluma | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | `baza-nása` | reading, writing, numbers, §03–§04 grammar |
| Secondary | `mi-nása` | full grammar, first domains, §05–§14 |
| Higher | `sula-nása` | domain registers, §15–§19, translation |
| Lifelong | `véta-nása` | corpus study, §24 validation work |

The full beginner-to-fluent path lives in `course/` (§22).

## 19.3 Culture and the arts

`arto` art, `muziko`, `filmo` film, `teatro`, `romano` novel, `poezio`,
`fabelo` tale, `kanto` song, `danko` dance, `pentraĵo` painting,
`skulptaĵo` sculpture, `ekspozisio` exhibition, `festivalo`, `verko` work. A
genre is `X-verko` or `X-tipo`; an artist is `X-or`/`X-isto`.

## 19.4 Everyday life

Home `nésa`, room `tiva`, door `tura`, table `kiva-tavi`, chair `tavi-jésa`,
food `fura`, meal `fura-míra`, water `nela`, money `gava`, work `kiva`,
rest `soma`, family `namo`. Greetings and politeness follow §09 society
patterns: `sala` *hello*, `ravi` *goodbye*, `kala` *welcome*, `nakos` *thanks*,
`noha` *sorry*, `téva` *please* (§6.8).

## 19.5 Social registers in daily use

Daily conversation is **Informal** (§23.1) with fusions (`mila`, `nésana`,
`wégai`), irregular pasts (`rá`, `dá`, `víra`), topic `ia`, and discourse
markers (`evi`, `koro`, `maka`). Written schoolwork, news, and reference use
**Standard**; law and ceremony use **Formal**; science and computing use
**Technical**. A fluent speaker switches register by audience, as §§23.1–23.3
prescribe.

## 19.6 Onomastics (naming people and places)

- Personal names are bare proper nouns: `Mila`, `Tuma`, `Séra`.
- Family names are the `namo` followed by `-la`: `Séra-la` *of the house of Séra*.
- Place names use `-ia`/`-a`: `Belumia`, `Espera`; foreign names are respelled
  by §15.10 (`Londona`, `Shangai`).
- A title precedes the name: `Hulor Séra`, `Kémor Tuma`.

## 19.7 Food, religion, sport, media

These fields are open: new foods are loans respelled (`kafe`, `susi`),
new dishes are compounds (`fura-X`), religions and sports keep their
international names respelled, and media follows §16.5. No register rule
changes; only §15 legality applies.

## 19.8 Register

Everyday texts are Informal or Standard per §23; the cultural fields of §19.3
may rise to a literary Standard (§23.4) but never need Formal unless
ceremonial.
