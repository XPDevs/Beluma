# §05 Nouns — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: **Normative.**

## 5.1 No gender, no case

Beluma nouns have **no grammatical gender** and **no case inflection**. Relations
that other languages mark with case are shown by word order and prepositions
(§11). `luno` is "cat" regardless of sex; sex is stated only when relevant, with
`bino` *male* / `ravo` *female* where the dictionary provides them, or with
`nira`/`sula` for young/great animals.

## 5.2 Number

- Singular: bare noun — `luno` *a cat*.
- Plural: **`-s`** after a vowel, **`-as`** after a consonant — `lunos` *cats*,
  `lavoét → lavoét-as` *puppies*.
- Number is marked once on the noun phrase head; modifiers do not agree
  (`béla lunos` *beautiful cats*).
- After a numeral, the plural is optional but preferred in Formal:
  `tres lunos` / `tres luno` *three cats*.
- Mass nouns (`nela` water, `fura` food, `gava` money) do not normally pluralise;
  a plural implies *kinds* or *portions*: `nelas` *waters/kinds of water*.
- Collective nouns are lexical, not grammatical: `fira-os` flock, `misi-os` a people.

## 5.3 Articles and definiteness

| Form | Meaning | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lé` | *the* (definite) | known/specific referent |
| `o` | *a / an* (indefinite) | new or non-specific referent |
| (none) | bare noun | generic, mass, or with a possessor/demonstrative |

- `lé luno` *the cat*; `o luno` *a cat*; `lunos` *cats (in general)*.
- Proper names and pronouns take no article: `Mila`, `tu`.
- The article precedes the whole noun phrase: `lé béla luno` *the beautiful cat*.
- Formal register requires an article wherever §5.3 allows one (§23.2).

## 5.4 Possession

The possessor takes **`-la`** and precedes the noun: `mi-la nésa` *my house*,
`hulor-la tiva` *the leader's room*. A possessor may itself be a phrase:
`lé kémor-la livo` *the teacher's lesson*. Possession does not stack with an
article on the possessor (`mi-la`, not `lé mi-la`).

## 5.5 The noun phrase (order)

Demonstrative + possessor + numeral + adjective(s) + **NOUN** + relative clause:

> `eka mi-la tres béla lunos ka sola` — *these three beautiful cats of mine that play*

- Demonstratives `eka` (this), `oka` (that near you), `ta` (that over there).
- The noun is the head and is never separated from its modifiers by the verb.
- A relative clause follows the noun (§03.7).

## 5.6 Common noun classes (semantic, not grammatical)

| Class | Typical head | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| persons | `misi`, `kivor`, `lini` | `misi` person, `kivor` worker |
| body | `kúna`, `tuma`, `vosa` | `kúna-sulo` brain |
| nature | `nela`, `loma`, `séra` | `nela-sula` ocean |
| artefacts | `kora`, `pira`, `tiva` | `kora-nira` bucket |
| places | `nása`, `tava`, `an` | `kéma-nása` school |
| abstracts | `-kosa`, `-sula`, zero-derived adj | `béla-kosa` beauty |

These classes guide compounding (§15.7): a new word's head should name the class
it belongs to.

## 5.7 Proper nouns

Proper names are capitalised (§2.3) and behave as bare nouns. Foreign proper
names are respelled by §15.10: *London* → `Londona` / *Shanghai* → `Shangai`.
Place names in `lands`/`-ia` take the usual plural and preposition rules.

## 5.8 Pronoun substitutes

A noun may be replaced by a pronoun (§04); Beluma freely drops a subject pronoun
that is clear from context, especially in Informal speech (§04.7, §23.2).
