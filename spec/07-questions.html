§07 Questions — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: Normative. Questions are formed by fronting a question word. There is

no subject–verb inversion anywhere in the language.

7.1 Yes/no questions

Put kvo in slot 3 (before tense, after un and the time adverb); everything else

stays exactly where it is:

StatementQuestionGloss
Tu gai.Kvo tu gai?Do you go?
Siva s na nésa.Kvo siva s na nésa?Is Siva at home?
Tu wé gai.Kvo tu wé gai?Will you go?
Tu un né gai.Kvo tu un né gai?Did you not go?
Tu kima gai.Kvo tu kima gai?Can you go?
  • Final mark: kwamorka.
  • Answers: kék = yes, non = no. Full-sentence answers are preferred in Formal: Kék, mi wé gai. / Non, mi un wé gai.
  • kvo never appears in statements; a declarative containing kvo is non-conformant.

7.2 Content (wh-) questions

The question word fronts the clause in slot 0/1 position — before the subject,

before un and tense. No other change.

WordMeaningExampleGloss
kwawhatKwa tu féma?What do you eat?
kvawhich / that (choice)Kva loma tu léva?Which tree do you love?
kvasiwhoKvasi s ta?Who is that?
kwaraswhereKwaras tu gai?Where do you go?
kvadowhenKvado lé fira wé hura?When will the bird fly?
kvasowhyKvaso tu un gai?Why don't you go?
havihowHavi tu diva ta?How do you do that?
  • kwa = what (thing), kva = which (selection from a set). Both are dictionary words; do not interchange them.
  • Question words front; if the answer is a prepositional place, the question still uses kwaras alone (no preposition doubling): Kwaras tu s? Where are you?
  • With negation and tense, order is unchanged: Kvaso tu un né gai? — Why didn't you go?

7.3 Alternative and tag questions

  • Alternative: ora between options: Kvo tu gai ora tu suda? — *Do you go or do you call?* — answers name the option: Tu gai.
  • Tag questions: Informal only, trailing koro? (lit. really?): Siva s gavo, koro? — Siva is good, isn't she? Formal text SHOULD end with a plain declarative or a kvo question instead (§6.9).
  • Echo/rhetorical questions repeat the other speaker's fronted word; this is a discourse feature of Informal speech (informative): Kwaras? Na lé tiva! Where? In the room!

7.4 Embedded questions

An embedded (indirect) question keeps ordinary statement order and takes

no kvo; the wh-word alone marks it:

  • Mi kéma kwaras tu gai. — I know where you go.
  • Mi un kéma kwa tu féma. — I don't know what you eat.
  • The embedded clause takes statement punctuation inside a larger sentence and a final dota at the end of the whole.

7.5 Question words vs relative words

English uses who/which/that for both jobs; Beluma keeps separate series:

JobSeriesExample
Questionkvasi, kwa, kva …Kvasi s ta? — Who is that?
Relativeka, ta, ka-laLé misi ka s unréva. — The person who is sad.

A question word always fronts its clause; a relative word always follows its

noun (§03.5). Neither can do the other's job.

7.6 Interrogative intonation (informative)

Yes/no questions rise at kwamorka; content questions fall. Echo questions rise.

Stress never moves to mark a question — the fronted particle does all the work.
