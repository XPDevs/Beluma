# §09 Adjectives — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: **Normative.**

## 9.1 Adjectives and agreement

An adjective qualifies a noun and is **invariant** — no number, gender, or case
agreement: `béla luno`, `béla lunos`, `béla misis` are all correct. Adjectives
are a subclass of verb-like quality words: `béla` may be read as *beautiful* or
*become/be beautiful* (§6.9 zero-copula uses).

## 9.2 Position

Standard position is **before the noun**, in the noun-phrase slot order of §5.5:

> demonstrative + possessor + numeral + **adjective(s)** + NOUN

- A single adjective normally precedes: `sulo kivor` *a strong worker*.
- Two adjectives stack head-nearest-last by closeness of meaning:
  `nira túna kora-nira` *a small round bucket*. "Small" is more inherent than
  "round", so it stands farther from the noun.
- An adjective after the noun is a **predicative** or emphatic reading
  (`lé kora-nira túna` *the bucket is round*), or a stylistic fronting of one
  adjective in Formal/literary prose (§23.4).
- Adjectives never take `-s`: `béla lunos` (not `bélas lunos`).

## 9.3 Predicative use (no copula required)

A quality word may stand as the predicate directly, with the copula `s` only
when tense or focus must be marked:

- `Lé luno béla.` *The cat (is) beautiful.*
- `Lé luno né s béla.` *The cat was beautiful.*
- `Lé luno s béla.` *The cat is beautiful* (marked present, emphasis).

## 9.4 Comparison

Comparison uses a **particle frame**, not adjectival inflection:

| Degree | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| comparative | `mésa ADJ ban X` = *more ADJ than X* | `mésa béla ban tu` *more beautiful than you* |
| equality | `ekva ADJ kom X` = *as ADJ as X* | `ekva sulo kom koro` *as strong as a bear* |
| superlative | `mésa ADJ ze X` = *the most ADJ of/among X* | `mésa gavo ze tous` *the best of all* |
| less | `mísa ADJ ban X` = *less ADJ than X* | `mísa kula ban tira` *less heavy than metal* |
| least | `mísa ADJ ze X` | `mísa gavo ze mis` |

- `ban` *than*; `kom` *as*; `ze` *of, among*; `mésa` *more*, `mísa` *less*.
- The standard of comparison follows `ban/kom/ze` as a noun phrase.
- Superlative may also be expressed by `mésa … ze toutes` or with the intensifier
  `vévlo` (§9.5).

## 9.5 Intensity

| Intensifier | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vévlo` | very, extremely | `vévlo sulo` *very strong* |
| `nira-vévlo` | slightly, a little | `nira-vévlo kula` *a bit heavy* |
| `mavé-sula` | too (excessively) | `mavé-sula túna` *too round* |
| `sufite` | enough | `sufite gavo` *good enough* |
| `tro` | too (informal) | `tro béla` *way too beautiful* |

Degree words precede the adjective: `vévlo nira túna` *very slightly round*.

## 9.6 Adjective derivation

Adjectives are built by §15.2.3: `-ik` (quality), `-oka` (having), `-uma`
(lacking), `-vela` (full of), `-sama` (like), `-ona` (made of), `-jan` (able),
`-jira` (worthy). A participle used adjectivally takes `-va/-ké` (§6.4):
`sola-va lini` *a playing child*, `fóra-ké loma` *a cut tree*.

## 9.7 Colour, size, age, value

Adjectives of colour (`bélu` system, §16), size (`nira`, `sula`), age (`ninva`
new, `néva` old), and value (`gavo`, `zu`) are ordinary adjectives with the
default order **value > size > age > colour > origin > material** before the
noun: `gavo nira ninva séra kora` *a good little new gold box*.

## 9.8 Nominalised adjectives

Any adjective may stand as a noun (§15.3): `lé nula` *the cold (one/thing)*,
`lé sulo` *the strong one*. Plural marking then applies to the phrase head:
`lé bélas` *the beautiful ones*. For abstract quality prefer the suffixed forms
`-kosa` / `-sula` (§15.4).
