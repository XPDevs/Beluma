§18 Government, Law, and Administration — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: Normative for patterns and style; the word list is Informative.

18.1 Style of the Formal register

Legal, governmental, and diplomatic Beluma is written in the Formal

register (§23.1): full morphology, mandatory accents, explicit articles, no

fusions, no ellipsis, no discourse markers, passive preferred. Legal texts are

the reference example of Formal Beluma.

18.3 Law and justice

juro law/right, tribunalo court, juĝisto judge, advokato lawyer,

prokuroro prosecutor, akuzito defendant, atestanto witness, verdikto

verdict, puno punishment, malliberejo prison, apelasio appeal,

kontrakto contract, rajto right, devo duty, kulpo guilt.

Roles follow jura-X: jura-kivor legal professional, jura-pira *legal

instrument. A verdict is dika-sa (judge-says*).

18.4 Reading a law (informative pattern)

A statute states **(1) subject authority, (2) obligation, (3) actor,

(4) action, (5) condition**:

Lé velta gida ke lé velta-misoj pagi dútas na máva-ona. —
The state requires that citizens pay taxes on the first of the month.

Formal Beluma prefers gida must and the passive s + V-ké + li (§6.5):

Lé dútas gida s pagi-ké li lé velta-misoj. —
Taxes must be paid by the citizens.

18.5 Military and security

milito war, armeo army, militisto soldier, ofisiro officer,

generalo, armilo weapon, defendo defence, atako attack, strategio,

taktiko, spionado espionage. An operation is X-operaco; a base is

X-bazo. The military register is a subset of Formal with Technical terms; it

avoids the passive for orders (imperative bare verb, §6.8).

18.6 Diplomacy and rights

diplomatio, diplomato, ambasado embassy, ambasadoro, traktato treaty,

interkonsento agreement, rajto right, libereko liberty, juro

justice. International instruments use the pattern X-deklaro (*declaration of

X): rajto-deklaro bill of rights*.

18.7 Numbering and dates in official texts

Official documents use the Formal date (§14.6), spelled-out numbers for legality

(unésa mava-unésa 11 November), and the ISO form in parentheses. Article

and section numbers use ordinals: Trósa artiklo, onaji sekcio *Article 3,

section 1*.
