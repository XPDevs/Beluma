§10 Adverbs — Beluma Language Standard v1.0

Status: Normative.

10.1 Manner adverbs (-lé)

A manner adverb is any adjective + -lé: gavo-lé well, sulo-lé

strongly, vira-lé quickly, gavo-nira-lé quite well. It follows the verb

(or its object) as the last element of the clause:

Mi gai nésa vira-lé. — I go home quickly.

Non-derived manner words also exist: tiel thus, so; alie otherwise;

finage finally.

10.2 Degree adverbs

Degree words modify adjectives and adverbs and precede them: vévlo very,

nira-vévlo slightly, mavé-sula too, sufite enough, tro too (informal),

preskawe almost. Chain: vévlo nira-vévlo kula very slightly heavy.

Safa, mi né gai tava. — Yesterday I went to the market.

10.4 Place and direction adverbs

eka here, oka there (near you), ta over there; tie in that place;

kie where; sirka around; for away; heme home(ward); supo up,

subo down, sende outside? — direction is normally a preposition phrase

(§11): na at, a to, de from.

10.5 Sentence adverbs

These judge the whole clause and normally stand first:

vento perhaps, nur only, tamen however, kiale therefore,

ekzemple for example, almenawe at least, zira` fine/OK (as a hedge)*.

10.6 Position summary

Front slot (sentence adverbs) → time slot → bare verb + object → manner -lé.

When two adverbs compete for the same position, the more specific stands nearer

the verb. Adverbs are invariant; they never take plural or possession marking.
